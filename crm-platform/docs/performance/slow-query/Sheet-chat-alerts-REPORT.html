Verdict: Partly met. Critical, Other and Slow Queries spaces are wired (bridge, watcher, Alertmanager config, rules, compose service, runbook). The Deployments space and the Grafana contact points are built on a separate branch and are not in this report.

What was built

A small Node process (tools/chat-bridge) receives Alertmanager webhooks and posts cards to Google Chat. The same process polls Postgres every 15 s with a read-only role and posts slow queries with all literals stripped. Everything is optional: the service starts only when asked.

How it was tested

The real bridge and watcher process run against a fake Chat receiver and a throwaway database with pg_stat_statements (dev container). Checks: routing critical / warning / resolved / slow_query, Watchdog dropped but counted, big payload split, 5xx and 429 retried, permanent failure gives 502 without crashing, pg_sleep(7) gives one post with no email, phone, token or number, a statement delta post, burst limited to 10 per minute followed by a digest, secrets absent from logs and /metrics. Also promtool, amtool on all four Alertmanager files, docker compose config, bash -n and the runbook ratchet.

Update: autonomy, accuracy and resolver (measured)

Changes since the first version: three spaces only (Critical, Other, Deployments; slow queries go to Other and escalate to Critical at 60 s or a blocker holding a waiter for 30 s), one Chat thread per alert with "RESOLVED after N" replies, runbook diagnosis with live Prometheus evidence, classified slow-query alerts (slow / blocked / idle in transaction, with allow-list), and an incident resolver (cancel runaway SELECT, end idle-in-transaction blocker, db-pool, code-fix ticket file, service stubs) that defaults to dry-run.

Accuracy harness (real watcher, read-only role, throwaway database, 3 trials, thresholds scaled to 3 s / 8 s / 4 s, poll 1 s): precision 1.000, recall 1.000 (TP 18, FN 0, FP 0).

Not proven: real Google Chat threading behaviour, production Postgres, the statement path under a quiet pg_stat_statements (recurring-statement detection was not measurable on this busy shared server; its logic is covered only by the resolver code-fix check, not by a measured detection rate).

What this does NOT prove

  • Delivery to real Google Chat and how the cards look there.
  • The real production Postgres, its role grants, or pg_stat_statements on it.
  • Memory under production load; the pg module resolution inside the real desk-api image.
  • Grafana contact points, the Grafana rule and dashboard, and the deploy-main notifier (other branch).
  • The full alerting and health-readiness runners were not re-run (apps/api is not built here); only their promtool and ratchet parts were.
